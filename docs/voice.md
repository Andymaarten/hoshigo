# hoshigo voice

The living guide to how hoshigo sounds, on the site and in every email.
Kept by Yuki. When the owner corrects something, it goes in the feedback log
at the bottom, and the rules above get updated to match.

## In one line

A quiet, warm person who likes you, says little, and now and then lets a
small bit of magic through the door.

## Principles

1. **Plain first.** Most sentences are just clear. The voice lives in the
   one line that isn't.
2. **One light touch per message.** An email, a dialog, an empty state: one
   smile at most. If two lines are trying to be charming, cut one.
3. **Clarity is never the joke.** Buttons, errors, expiry times and "not you?"
   lines are plain. Humour sits around the functional part, never inside it.
4. **Short.** If a sentence works without a word, drop the word.
5. **A little magical, never cute.** The world outside the screen: doors,
   keeping, a place set aside for you. Not sparkles, not wizards.
6. **Human, not corporate.** Talk like one person to one person. No
   reassurance language, no marketing verbs.
7. **Calm about mistakes.** Errors say what happened and what to do next, in
   that order, without apology theatre.
8. **We would like you gone within minutes.** Copy never tries to keep
   people around. No "don't miss out", no nudges to come back.

## The owner's examples (the gold standard, verbatim)

- Magic link email subject: "Your magic link to enter the world of hoshigo."
- Instead of "Confirm your email address": "Is it you?" / "Hello?" / "Is it
  really you?" / "Hoshi would like to confirm this before someone else starts
  giving out five stars on your behalf..."

What makes these work: a question instead of a command; a real reason given
with a straight face; one imagined world ("enter the world of"), not five.

## House rules

- Never a literal hyphen `-` in user visible copy. Use an em dash `—` or
  rephrase ("five star page", "log in", "sign up").
- No emoji. Anywhere.
- "hoshigo" is always lowercase, even at the start of a sentence.
- English copy, British or American spelling is fine but stay consistent
  within one screen.
- Headings on the site are lowercase and end with a full stop when they are
  a statement ("your friends.", "nothing here.").
- Buttons say what happens: "Choose a new password", not "Continue" or
  "Submit" where a verb can be specific.
- Sparing exclamation marks. The default is zero.

## Do and don't

| Don't | Do | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirm your email address | Is it really you? | A question from a person, not an instruction from a system. |
| Oops! Something went wrong. | That didn't go through. Try again in a moment. | Says what happened and what to do. No "oops". |
| Unlock your personalized journey! | Keep your own five star page. | No marketing verbs. |
| We're so excited to have you! | (nothing, or) Your page is ready. | Excitement is claimed, not shown. |
| Rest assured, your data is safe. | Only you and your friends see this. | Concrete beats reassurance. |
| Discover, curate and share your favorites. | Keep the handful of things you'd give five stars. | No rule of three lists. |
| Stars aligned! Your star-studded list is out of this world! | Added. | Stacked puns. Also a hyphen. |
| Hoshi says hi! Hoshi saved your note! Hoshi misses you! | (Hoshi appears once, where confirming or keeping matters.) | Hoshi is rare, or Hoshi is a mascot. |
| Link expired, as all good things do... | That link has expired. Ask for a new one below. | Functional lines stay plain. |
| Didn't request this? No worries! | Didn't ask for this? Ignore it. The door closes by itself. | Calm and useful, one small image. |

## Words to avoid

Oops, whoops, uh oh, awesome, amazing, excited, unlock, journey, elevate,
seamless, curate, discover (as a verb in taglines), empower, leverage,
community (as a selling point), personalized, rest assured, don't worry,
simply, just (as in "just click"), feed (for what friends added; prefer
"latest from your friends").

## Words we like

keep, handful, five stars, quiet, place, page, door, press (not click or
tap), friends, real people, latest, a line, a few words.

## Glossary

- **hoshigo**: the site. Japanese: *hoshi*, star, and *go*, five. Always
  lowercase.
- **a hoshigo**: one listing, one thing someone gives five stars. Plural:
  **hoshigos** (no apostrophe).
- **five stars**: the only rating there is. If it's on a page, it's five
  stars. We never talk about four.
- **Hoshi**: a quiet presence, the keeper of the stars. Not a mascot, never
  drawn, never chatty. Appears where something is being confirmed or kept
  safe. At most once per message, and not in every message.
- **page**: a person's hoshigo profile (hoshigo.cc/name). Prefer "page" over
  "profile" in user copy.
- **friends**: people who see everything on each other's page.
- **the red stamp**: the round red button that adds a hoshigo.

## Emails

- Subject: a question or a short line that sounds like a person. The owner's
  magic link subject is the model.
- Heading: short, often the same as or an echo of the subject.
- Body: one or two sentences. The light touch goes here.
- Button: the plain action.
- Fine print: that it works once, when it expires, and a copyable link.
- Footer: "Not you?" / "Didn't ask for this?" and what happens if they do
  nothing.
