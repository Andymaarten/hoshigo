# Sharing a listing

How one five star thing leaves hoshigo and brings a few quiet people back. Written with a social media strategist's and an Instagram curator's eye, then filtered through what hoshigo is: no ads, no algorithm, no AI, gone within minutes.

## Where people will share, and in what shape

| Where | Format | What we make |
|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp, iMessage, Signal, Telegram, X, LinkedIn, Bluesky, Mastodon, Slack | Link preview (Open Graph) | 1200 x 630 image + title + description, generated per listing |
| Instagram Stories | 1080 x 1920 image | Downloadable / shareable Story card |
| Instagram feed | 1080 x 1350 (portrait) | Downloadable feed card. Portrait takes more of the screen than square, so it wins over 1080 x 1080 |

The link preview matters most. Most sharing of a single recommendation happens in private chats ("you have to watch this"), and there the preview card *is* the design. Instagram Stories come second: it is where people show taste, and a Story cannot carry a clickable link unless you add a link sticker, so the card has to name the address in plain text.

Skip for now: Reels/TikTok (video, and a poor fit for "a still thing I love"), Pinterest, hashtags.

## What the card contains

1. **The photo or cover, big.** It is the reason anyone stops. Never crop it into something unrecognisable; show it whole on a paper ground.
2. **Title and by**, in Newsreader, like on the profile.
3. **The note**, in italic, clipped to two or three lines. The note is what makes this personal and not an ad. If there is no note, leave the space empty rather than filling it.
4. **Whose it is**: the person's name, small.
5. **The mark**: the wordmark with the red dot, and `hoshigo.cc/handle` written out.

Leave out: star ratings or "5/5" graphics (the whole site is five stars, saying it again is noise), counts of any kind, "trending", emoji, QR codes, the word "app", calls to "download".

## The copy

The owner's draft: *"I track all my five-star listings on hoshigo. Will you join me?"*

Honest critique:
- "Track" and "listings" are productivity and real estate words. hoshigo is about keeping, not tracking; about things, not listings.
- "Five-star" contains a hyphen, which is against house style.
- "Will you join me?" puts a sales pitch in the sharer's mouth. People share taste gladly, but they hesitate to share something that recruits their friends. That hesitation means fewer shares.
- It is the longest thing on the card, and it is not about the thing being shared.

Proposal, on the card (English, short):

> **hoshıgo** 星五 · Japanese for five stars
> hoshigo.cc/andymaarten

That is all the brand needs to say. The curiosity ("what is hoshigo?") does the work, and the red dot is recognisable after two or three sightings.

The invitation belongs on the page the link opens, where the visitor has already chosen to look:

> One of Andy's five stars.
> *What are yours?* Keep your own, a handful per list. Free, no ads, no algorithm.

For the native share sheet text: *"One of my five stars: Title, by Someone. hoshigo.cc/…"* The sharer can edit it before sending.

## Staying true to the identity

- **Share only what the person chose to share**, and only what is publicly visible on their page.
- **No tracking parameters**, no referral codes, no "invite 3 friends", no share counts, no nudges to share after adding something. The Share button waits in the listing sheet and does not ask for attention.
- **One image, well made**, is better than a template gallery. Everyone's cards look alike on purpose, so that across many shares the same paper ground and red dot become the brand.
- The page a share opens should answer three questions and then let people go: what is this, whose is it, how do I make mine.

## Later: sharing a whole profile

Same grammar at a larger scale: paper ground, the name set big like the profile heading ("Andy."), a strip of three to five covers from one list, the wordmark at the bottom. One card per list ("Andy's five films") works better than one crowded card for everything. The Open Graph image for `/handle` can reuse the same components.
